The attrited cohort has no Q4 customers, so LMDI is undefined for it and the cell uses the sequential fallback. The whole change is a customers effect: their Q1 spend of $1.45M leaves the book.

Note the difference from the portfolio bridge for scenario B. The portfolio-level customers effect is -$1.83M, which is larger than the $1.45M the attrited customers actually spent in Q1. The portfolio identity prices every lost customer at the portfolio average, and attrited customers spent 32.7% less per head than existing customers. The offset shows up as smaller negative frequency and ticket effects, because the customers who remain are heavier spenders on average. Both views add up to the same total.

All four methods add up to the same total. They differ in how they share out the interaction between factors. Sequential substitution values each step at the levels left by the steps before it, so the answer depends on the order. LMDI uses one common weight, the log mean of Q1 and Q4 spend, for every factor, so there is no order to choose. The average over all 24 orders lands close to LMDI, which is the expected behaviour.
